Accelerating Therapeutic Advances with Real‑World Evidence

ORBIT brings together investigators across AI, economics, statistics, data science, population health, and clinical medicine to develop methods and tools that harness real‑world data for more efficient medical evidence generation and smarter, faster clinical trials.

About ORBIT

There is an unmet need for more efficient approaches to medical evidence generation that can inform physicians, policymakers, business, and the public. ORBIT focuses on two core objectives: (1) developing techniques and tools to use real‑world data to answer questions that might otherwise require randomized controlled trials; and (2) making randomized trials more efficient by overlaying observational data with advances in AI and data science.

Objectives

  • Methodological Innovation — Develop and validate causal inference methods and trial‑augmentation designs using observational data.
  • Training & Workforce — Seminars, workshops, and fellowships to upskill investigators in RWE and AI.
  • Data & Tools — Curated datasets, reproducible code, and open‑source tools to accelerate evidence generation.
  • Ethics & Governance — Privacy, fairness, transparency, and patient engagement across projects.

Apply for ORBIT Funding

Overview

  • Funding tracks: Methods, Pilot Studies, Infrastructure/Tools.
  • Typical award: $25,000–$100,000 for 6–12 months.
  • Review criteria: Significance, Innovation, Rigor, Team, Feasibility, Impact on RWE.

Eligibility

  • Lead PI: faculty or research staff; trainees may apply with a mentor.
  • Include a NIH-aligned Data Management & Sharing plan.
  • IRB approval (or exemption) required before start for human data.

Timeline

  1. Notice: January 15
  2. LOI: February 15
  3. Proposals: March 31
  4. Decisions: May 15
  5. Start: July 1

Requirements

  • Open science where feasible.
  • Quarterly updates and final report.
  • Acknowledge ORBIT and DCRI.

FAQ

What types of projects are a good fit?
Causal inference with observational data, AI‑enabled trial augmentation, pragmatic trial design, and enabling infrastructure.
Do I need preliminary data?
Not required for methods/infrastructure tracks; helpful for pilot studies.
What about human subjects protections?
IRB approval or exemption before start, HIPAA compliance, and governance plans.
